Gas solubility: Henry's law, and how it changes with temperature

Why does warm soda go flat faster than cold soda?

A sealed bottle of soda holds carbon dioxide two ways: dissolved in the drink, and as gas in the space at the top. The two trade molecules until they balance, and where they balance depends on temperature: warm water holds less gas. Open the bottle and the gas above escapes, so the dissolved gas comes out to replace it, as fizz.

Run, with the bottle sealed, and let the gas settle between drink and headspace (a minute). Warm it: gas comes out of the drink into the space above; cool it, and it goes back in. Then open the bottle, cold, and warm.

Why gas dissolves at all, and less when warm

A CO₂ molecule in the drink is surrounded by water molecules that pull on it: down there its energy is lower than in the gas above, by roughly the depth of that pull. In equilibrium, by Boltzmann, it is found in the drink more often than above by a factor of about eΔE/kT, times how much room it has there. That factor is Henry's law: the dissolved amount is proportional to the gas's pressure above, with a constant that falls as kT rises, because the same energy difference counts for less when everything is shaking harder. Measured here: the drink is 5.6 times as crowded with CO₂ as the space above at kT 0.40, 4.8 times at 0.425, and only 2.8 times at 0.45. (For real CO₂ in water, the solubility halves between 0 and 25 °C.)

Why opening the bottle makes it fizz

Sealed, the headspace fills with CO₂ until the balance is struck. Opened, that gas drifts away and is replaced by air, and the drink, now holding far more than the balance with the air allows, gives up its gas. In a real drink it comes out mostly as bubbles, which grow on scratches and specks (it takes a seed to start a bubble); here it simply evaporates from the surface. Warm, it starts with less, but loses it faster.

Caveats

  • 2D Lennard-Jones "water" with a gas it attracts (as deeply as it attracts itself) but which does not attract itself; real CO₂ also reacts a little with water (carbonic acid), which does not change the story.
  • A heat bath acts on every molecule to hold the temperature you set; it slows the molecules' wandering, so how fast the gas comes out is only roughly right; how much is dissolved is not affected.
  • The opened bottle is a rule of the page: CO₂ more than 15 σ above the drink is turned into air (it has drifted away into the room), every 5 τ.
