Enthalpy and the Gibbs free energy

Why do chemists talk about enthalpy instead of energy? And what is so special about the Gibbs free energy?

Where the heat goes

In the locked box every bit of heat goes into the molecules' energy: Q = ΔU. In the free one the gas expands as it warms, pushing the lid and its weight up, and that work, F·Δh = pΔV, also has to come out of the heat. For a 2D ideal gas exactly half does: it takes 2k of heat per degree per molecule at constant pressure, against 1k at constant volume (measured natively: 1.95 and 1.01). So the free one warms half as much.

Chemistry happens in open beakers, at the atmosphere's constant pressure, so the heat a reaction gives out or takes in is never just ΔU: the products push the atmosphere back (or let it in) on the way. Rather than carry pΔV around in every calculation, chemists add it to the energy once and for all: the enthalpy H = U + pV. At constant pressure the heat is exactly ΔH, so tables of "heats of reaction" are tables of ΔH. At constant volume (a sealed bomb calorimeter) the heat is ΔU. "Heat" on its own is not a property of anything: it is energy on its way in or out, and how much is needed depends on what else the system does meanwhile.

Why the Gibbs free energy

What decides whether something happens by itself is the second law: the total entropy, of the system and its surroundings, must rise. At constant temperature and pressure the surroundings' change is simple: they give the system heat ΔH, at temperature T, so their entropy changes by −ΔH/T (the sneeze: phase transitions, part 3). The total change is ΔS − ΔH/T = −(ΔH − TΔS)/T = −ΔG/T. So "G goes down" is the second law for the whole world, written using only the system's own quantities: the Gibbs free energy G = H − TS is the system's bookkeeping for the surroundings' entropy. That is why it appears everywhere in chemistry and biology, where nearly everything happens at fixed temperature and pressure: in reactions, in folding, in boiling (the phase with the lowest G wins), in binding (ΔG of binding is measured, and predicted on the FEP page).

And the entropy theme: in G = H − TS the entropy is multiplied by T, so the hotter it is, the more entropy decides, and the more a process can afford to take in heat (ΔH > 0) if it gains entropy.

Caveats

  • A nearly ideal 2D gas of repulsive molecules; a 3D ideal gas has cV = 3/2 and cp = 5/2.
  • The free lid has no friction, so a sudden heat burst sets it bouncing, and the bounce stores energy that comes back slowly; heat slowly to see the textbook numbers.
  • The heater adds heat in exactly counted amounts by speeding every molecule up by the same factor.
