Molecular motors: the Brownian ratchet

How does a molecule walk, when everything around it is shaking it at random?

Inside a cell, kinesin carries cargo along a protein track, 8 nanometres a step, a hundred steps a second, through a storm of molecular collisions that would knock anything that size about by more than a step every microsecond. It does not fight the storm: it uses it. It lets the jiggling carry it about and, with energy from ATP, keeps only the jiggles that go forwards.

Rectifying noise

A molecule can't push off from anything: anything it might push against is being knocked about as hard as it is. What it can do is change its grip. While the grip is off, a motor diffuses equally forwards and backwards. When the grip switches on, the lopsided potential sorts them: those that drifted forwards past the nearby crest roll into the groove ahead, and only the rare ones that drifted all the way back past the far crest roll back. On average, a step forwards per few cycles: the flashing ratchet (Ajdari and Prost, 1992; Astumian, 1997).

It needs both ingredients. With the grip always on, it is a ratchet with nothing to power it, and thermal noise alone cannot make it go one way (that would be a perpetual-motion machine of the second kind: Feynman's ratchet and pawl, Lectures I, ch. 46). With the grip always off there is nothing to rectify. And it needs the noise: freeze the motors and they sit in their grooves. Too hot, and the grip cannot hold them. The fuel's job is to switch the grip, paying for the order the sorting creates; each switch-on lifts the motors up the slope they then slide down, which is the energy that ends as heat.

Real kinesin is a two-headed walker whose heads change their grip on the microtubule as they bind ATP and release ADP; it burns about 20 kT per 8 nm step and stalls under a load of about 6 piconewtons. The principle is the same: chemistry switching the grip, and thermal motion doing the moving. This blind ratchet is far more wasteful (about 60 kT a step, and it stalls under a load of 1 to 2 kT per step), because it switches on a clock, whatever the motors are doing; kinesin's chemistry waits for its free head to land ahead before it lets go behind.

Caveats

  • Not molecular dynamics: overdamped Langevin dynamics of independent motors in a sawtooth potential (peak 5 kT at room temperature, its crest a fifth of the way along each step), switched on and off. The surrounding water is a heat bath: friction plus random kicks.
  • Units: a step is the track's period; time is in units of a step's diffusion time.
  • Fuel burnt is the potential energy the grip gives the motors when it switches on (the least the chemistry must supply).

About this simulation

  • Method: overdamped Langevin dynamics (Brownian dynamics, JavaScript) of 30 independent motors in 1D: each step moves a motor by (force / friction) Δt plus a random step √(2D Δt).
  • Ensemble: a heat bath (the water) at the temperature you set; the grip's switching puts energy in, which ends up in the bath.
  • Size: 30 motors; time step 10⁻⁴ (a step's diffusion time is 1).
