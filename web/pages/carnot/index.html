Heat engines and heat pumps

Why is the Carnot engine the most efficient possible? And how can a heat pump beat 100%?

This cylinder of gas is a real (if tiny) heat engine. Its walls can be connected to a hot bath, a cold bath, or insulated; a piston lets it do work. Run the Carnot cycle and the page books every joule of heat and work, molecule collision by molecule collision.

The cycle

  1. Hot, expanding. The walls touch the hot bath; the gas expands, pushing the piston and doing work, while heat Qh flows in to keep it hot.
  2. Insulated, expanding. The walls are insulated; the gas keeps expanding and cools down to the cold temperature, doing more work out of its own energy.
  3. Cold, compressing. The walls touch the cold bath; the piston pushes the gas back, and heat Qc flows out into the cold bath.
  4. Insulated, compressing. Insulated again; compression heats the gas back up to the hot temperature, ready to start over.

Over one cycle the gas returns to where it started, so the net work out is W = Qh − Qc, and the efficiency is W / Qh.

Why nothing can beat it

Heat Q flowing in at temperature T brings entropy Q/T. The gas ends each cycle exactly as it began, so it cannot keep any entropy: everything that came in with Qh at Th must leave with Qc at Tc, at least, Qc/Tc ≥ Qh/Th. So some heat must always be dumped, and the best possible efficiency is

ηmax = 1 − Tc / Th

The Carnot cycle reaches this when it runs infinitely slowly: heat then only ever flows between things at the same temperature, which creates no entropy. Run the piston faster and the gas lags behind the walls (it is a little colder than the hot bath while taking in heat, a little hotter than the cold bath while giving it out), and the piston stirs the gas: entropy is created, and the efficiency drops below the limit. That is why a real engine, which must deliver power and so cannot run slowly, never reaches it. Weaken the thermal contact and the lag gets worse at any speed.

Heat pumps

Run the same cycle backwards and work goes in, heat Qc is taken from the cold side, and heat Qh = Qc + W is delivered to the hot side. A heat pump's figure of merit is how much heat it delivers per unit of work, Qh/W, and the same entropy argument limits it to Th/(Th − Tc): with the temperatures here, several times more heat than work. Nothing is created from nothing: the extra heat is pumped out of the cold side. It also explains why an air conditioner or heat pump struggles in a heat wave or deep cold: the bigger the temperature difference, the lower the limit.

Caveats

  • A 2D gas of 800 small molecules. The heat bath acts on every molecule (each has a chance per unit time, the “thermal contact”, of being given a fresh velocity at the bath's temperature): heat flowing in only through the walls would make cycles very slow. Every bit of heat exchanged is booked exactly. Single cycles are noisy; trust the average of several.
  • For this 2D gas an insulated expansion cools it as T × area = constant; the cycle's turning points are set accordingly.
