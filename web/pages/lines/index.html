The evidence for atoms: Michelson, 1892; Buisson and Fabry, 1912

Why are a hot gas's spectral lines blurred? And how did that show hydrogen glows as atoms?

Every atom of hydrogen gives out light of exactly the same red colour. Yet a tube of glowing hydrogen shows its red line smeared out, and more so the hotter it is. The atoms are moving: each one coming towards you looks a little bluer, each one going away a little redder (the Doppler shift), and the line is the spread of their speeds.

Run, and watch the lines settle. Heat the gas and they widen; cool it and they sharpen. Hydrogen's line is four times wider (relative to its colour) than oxygen's: √16. Drag a box on the gas and shove it towards you, and the lines from that part shift blue.

A thermometer and a balance in one

An atom moving towards you at speed v shifts its light's wavelength by Δλ/λ = −v/c. In a gas the speeds along your line of sight spread with a Gaussian of width √(kT/m), so the line does too: its width is λ √(kT/mc²), up to a factor (2.355 for the full width at half height). Knowing the temperature, the width weighs the atom that glows; knowing the atom, it measures the temperature, which is how astronomers take the temperature of a star's atmosphere.

Perrin (Atoms, §38) cites Buisson and Fabry's measurements (1912): the widths agreed with the kinetic theory to about a percent, for helium, neon and krypton, and for hydrogen they came out as for a mass of 1, not 2: the red line comes from hydrogen atoms, not molecules. Try the "mass from the width" readout: the light atoms here come out at 1, the heavy ones at 16.

Caveats

  • The atoms do not really emit: the page takes each one's velocity along x and draws the colour it would show, in laboratory units (the simulation's kT = 1 is 1,000 K, its unit of mass 1 u).
  • Real lines also have a natural width and are broadened by collisions (pressure broadening); at low pressure and high temperature Doppler's is the largest.
  • 2D molecules: the line of sight is one of the two directions, so the widths are exactly those of 3D.
