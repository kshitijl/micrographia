Why do reactions roughly double in speed every 10 °C?

Cooking, spoiling, insulin going off in a hot car: all are molecules getting over an energy barrier. Here each small box holds a sticky pocket with one molecule caught in it. To escape it has to collect a big kick of energy from its jostling neighbours. Warm the boxes a little and count how much more often molecules get out.

Rates are Boltzmann factors

A molecule in the pocket sits in an energy well. Getting out needs an energy Ea above its usual share, and the chance of having that much extra energy at any instant is the Boltzmann factor e−Ea/kT (the energy of one molecule is spread exponentially). So the escape rate is k = A e−Ea/kT, Arrhenius's law. Plot ln k against 1/T and the points fall on a line whose slope is −Ea.

The toy barrier is a few ε, comparable to kT, so escapes happen often enough to count in a few seconds. Chemical and biological barriers are much higher: 50 kJ/mol is 20 kT at room temperature. A 10 °C rise is only 3% more kT, but multiplied by 20 in the exponent that becomes a factor of e0.66 ≈ 2. That is the rule of thumb, and why it is only a rule: it holds for barriers near 50 kJ/mol.

The same arithmetic explains the pressure cooker. At 120 °C instead of 100 °C, the reactions that soften beans and cook meat run several times faster; in Leh, where water boils at 87 °C, they run several times slower. And it is why insulin and mRNA vaccines are kept cold: the reactions that degrade them are slowed by the same factor.

What the simulation adds is that nothing here is put in by hand. The escape rate comes out of Newton's laws for a few tens of thousands of molecules; the exponential dependence on temperature is a consequence of how energy is shared, not an assumption. The barrier read from the slope is not exactly the pocket's depth: the molecule also has to push solvent aside, and the prefactor A depends on temperature too (how often the solvent kicks), so the line bends slightly, steeper at the cold end. Real Arrhenius plots bend for the same reasons.
