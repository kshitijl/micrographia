Boltzmann and Maxwell

Why is a molecule's most likely energy zero, but its most likely speed not?

Boltzmann says the lower an energy, the likelier it is: the single most likely kinetic energy is zero. Maxwell says the most likely speed is a definite, nonzero one. Both are right, about the same molecules. The answer is in how many ways there are to have a given speed, and you can see it: below, every molecule's velocity is a dot. The densest point is not the most crowded ring

Look at the dots. They are densest at the centre, zero velocity: Boltzmann's rule, e−E/kT, says each small patch of velocities is less likely the faster it is, so the patch at zero is the likeliest of all. But a speed is not a patch, it is a whole ring: every direction at that speed. A ring near the centre is tiny and holds few dots, however dense; a ring further out is bigger, and its size grows with its radius faster than the density falls, at first. So the number of molecules at a speed rises, peaks, and then falls when the e−E/kT wins. Drag the ring and watch its count against its density. That peak is Maxwell's most likely speed: √(kT/m) in 2D, √(2kT/m) in 3D.

Energy is ½mv², so in 2D a thin ring of speeds is also a thin band of energies of the same area at every energy: the number of molecules at an energy just follows the density, and the likeliest energy is zero. In 3D the shells of a given energy grow with √E, so the likeliest energy is ½kT, not zero, though it is still the density e−E/kT of each state that falls. The rule "lower energy is likelier" is about single states; how many states there are at each energy (the density of states) is the other half of every distribution.

The average energy

The readout "average energy per molecule" answers the other question: ½kT for every direction a molecule can move, kT in 2D, (3/2)kT in 3D, whatever the molecule's mass (see heavy and light molecules). That is in fact how temperature is defined here: kT is read from the average energy. The histograms then test the rest: the shape of the whole distribution, which nothing forced, comes out as Boltzmann and Maxwell predicted. Heat or cool the gas, or kick some molecules, and watch the shape spoil and then return within a few τ.

Caveats

  • A dense-ish gas of repulsive molecules: the distributions of velocities are exactly Maxwell's at any density (velocities and positions are independent in equilibrium), so a liquid would show the same.
  • The energy here is kinetic energy only.
