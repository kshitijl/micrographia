Press Run, then click in each box. Molecules are coloured by their energy, so you see the sneeze as a hot spot that spreads. The entropy jumps a little at the click and keeps rising, slowly, while the heat spreads through the box. To compare with Q/T at once, sneeze everywhere: the same heat shared by every molecule at once, with nothing left to spread.

What the boxes show

The same sneeze, the same amount of heat Q, raises the library's entropy far more than the concert's: about Q/T in each, so four times more in a box four times colder. That is dS = dQ/T, measured: the entropy is computed from how the molecules' energies are spread (Boltzmann's H), the sneeze is counted exactly, and T is the box's temperature.

Why: counting ways

Entropy counts the ways the molecules could be arranged, S = k ln W. Adding energy always adds ways: with more energy to share, there are more ways to share it. That is why S rises with E (and when it doesn't, as in a few exotic systems with a ceiling on their energy, "negative temperatures" appear). For N molecules of a 2D gas, W grows as EN, so S = Nk ln E + (terms that do not depend on E), and dS/dE = Nk/E = 1/T, because E = NkT. The slope of S against E is 1/T: that is what temperature means.

And the slope falls as E grows: the curve of S against E flattens. In a cold box, the energy is shared among few ways, and a little more energy multiplies them a lot: a big jump in entropy. In a hot box it is already shared many ways, and the same bit adds relatively few. A sneeze in a quiet library makes a big disturbance; at a rock concert, nobody notices.

Spreading makes entropy too

A sneeze in one spot first adds less entropy than Q/T, and the rest arrives as the heat spreads. The spreading is irreversible: the hot molecules share their energy with the cold ones and it never gathers in the spot again. When the heat has spread everywhere the box has gained Q/T, the same as if every molecule had been warmed at once; the difference along the way is entropy made by heat flowing from the hot spot to its cooler surroundings. (A small sneeze into a few molecules makes them very fast, and they carry it across the thin gas quickly.)

Why it matters: heat flows from hot to cold

Move Q from the concert to the library and the concert loses Q/Thot of entropy, the library gains Q/Tcold, more. The total rises, so it happens by itself; the reverse would lower it, so it does not (except for brief, small fluctuations: can heat flow from cold to hot?). The same bookkeeping, with the surroundings' −ΔH/T as the sneeze, is behind the Gibbs free energy (enthalpy and G) and behind boiling and melting (phase transitions).

Caveats

  • The entropy here is the part that comes from the molecules' kinetic energies. The boxes keep their size and the gas is thin, nearly ideal, so the positions' part hardly changes. (In a denser gas some of the heat goes into the molecules' potential energy, and this part of the entropy then gains a little less than Q/T.) It is measured from a histogram, so it carries a little noise.
  • Q/T holds for a small Q; for a big sneeze the temperature rises during it, and the exact gain is Nk ln(1 + Q/E), a little less (measured, for Q = 300 into the library's 6,000 molecules: 569 k, against Nk ln(1 + Q/E) = 572 and Q/T = 600).
  • No heat bath: each box conserves its energy, apart from the sneezes, which are counted.
