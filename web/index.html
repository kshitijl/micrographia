Micrographia

Questions about heat, entropy and everyday physics, each answered with a live simulation of the molecules involved, running on your GPU.

  • Why can't you un-stir cream from coffee, when every collision can run backwards?
    Exact time reversal, and what one flipped bit does to it.
  • Could all the air in your room rush into one corner? How long would you wait?
    768 boxes of gas counting how often every molecule is on one side.
  • Where does the Boltzmann distribution come from?
    Boxes of gas sharing energy through their walls find e−E/kT from any start, and entropy rises.
  • Why is a molecule's most likely energy zero, but its most likely speed not?
    Every molecule's velocity as a dot, in 2D and 3D: Boltzmann's density of states against the number of them at each speed.
  • At the same temperature, does a heavy molecule have less energy than a light one?
    Equipartition, from light molecules to bowling balls, and Brownian motion.
  • Why is dS = dQ/T? Why does entropy rise with energy, and why is a sneeze in a library worse than at a rock concert?
    Sneeze heat into a cold box and a hot one, and measure the entropy each gains.
  • Can heat ever flow from cold to hot?
    128 tiny systems between two heat baths, and the fluctuation theorem.
  • Why don't oil and water mix?
    Attraction, entropy and temperature in a separating mixture.
  • Why is osmosis an entropic force? Where does the push actually come from?
    A semi-permeable membrane, solute molecules bouncing off it, and van 't Hoff's law.
  • Why does a bike pump get hot, and a spray can cold?
    A piston doing work on a gas: the adiabat, fast compression, and heat-conducting walls.
  • Why does a stretched rubber band pull back, and why does it get warm?
    Polymer chains as entropic springs: a pull proportional to temperature.
  • Why does air pressure fall with height? And is it really colder up high? (And why do cumulus clouds have flat bases?)
    A gas column under gravity: exponential profiles per species, and a uniform temperature.
  • Why do chemists talk about enthalpy instead of energy? And what is so special about the Gibbs free energy?
    The same heat warms a gas under a locked lid twice as much as one under a weighted piston.
  • Why is the Carnot engine the most efficient possible? And how can a heat pump beat 100%?
    A gas engine run cycle by cycle, with every joule of heat and work booked.
  • Phase transitions: why does a liquid boil when it gets hot enough? (And why does a pressure cooker cook faster?)
    Evaporation as escape over an energy barrier, and the Clausius–Clapeyron line.
  • Why does salt dissolve in water? And why does more dissolve when it is hot?
    A crystal of two kinds of ion dissolving: energy against entropy, and solubility against temperature.
  • Why does mixing salt into water lower its melting point?
    In 3D: a crystal under its liquid stops freezing when you pour in salt. Entropy of mixing, and road salt's limit.
  • Why does warm air hold more water? What does "saturated" mean?
    Water vapour over a pool: cool it and fog forms; hold a cold glass in it; breathe moist air in.
  • Why do liquid and vapour sit side by side, and why does the difference vanish at the critical point?
    Three faster ways than molecular dynamics, starting with a million-site lattice gas checked against Onsager's exact answer.
  • What happens, atom by atom, when a crystal melts? And why does a cooled liquid wait before it freezes?
    In 3D: a Lennard-Jones crystal heated and cooled in counted amounts; the melting plateau, supercooling, and the triple point (and why ice in a drink isn't at it).
  • Why do pollen grains jiggle in water? And how did that jiggling count atoms?
    Einstein, 1905, in 3D: follow a grain under the microscope, throw it, and find kT = D × friction.
  • Why does a cold diamond need almost no heat to warm it? And how does energy spread itself out?
    Einstein, 1907: quanta hopping between atoms; Boltzmann's distribution, S = k ln Ω, and heat capacity freezing out.
  • Why is the sky blue? And why does a fluid turn milky at its critical point?
    Light scattered by the unevenness of a fluid of molecules: blue from a random gas, white near the critical point.
  • Why is a gas just as sticky whatever its pressure? And how did that measure the size of a molecule?
    Maxwell, 1860; Loschmidt, 1865: stir a thin and a dense gas and watch the swirl die; the same viscosity.
  • Why do gases combine in whole-number volumes?
    Gay-Lussac, 1808; Avogadro, 1811: light sets off hydrogen and chlorine, and 1 + 1 volumes give 2, not Dalton's 1.
  • Why does it take more heat to warm some gases? Why don't argon atoms spin?
    Equipartition: atoms, molecules on springs and a crystal, heated alike; then the quanta that freeze spin and vibration.
  • Why are a hot gas's spectral lines blurred?
    Doppler: each atom's colour shifted by its speed; the width weighs the atom, and hydrogen glows as atoms.
  • Can you see the path of a single atom?
    Wilson's cloud chamber: aim and fire α particles; straight tracks, thickest at the end, now and then a sharp turn off a nucleus.
  • Thirteen ways to count atoms: do they agree?
    Perrin, 1913: his table of Avogadro's number, and yours, from the pages where you measured it.
  • Can a gas get hotter without its molecules getting faster? Is hot tea heavier?
    Einstein, 1905: a gas in a world where light is slow; speeds pile up below c, and heat has inertia.
  • Why does a hot stove glow red, not blue? Is light made of grains?
    Planck, 1900; Einstein, 1905: light's modes in a hot box, the ultraviolet catastrophe, and light knocking out electrons.
  • How did anyone count atoms one at a time?
    Radioactive flashes on a screen: Poisson's randomness, the half-life, and Rutherford's count of a gram.
  • How do we know electric charge comes in lumps?
    Millikan's oil drop: time its falls and rises, knock its charge with X-rays, and the charges come out in whole e's.
  • How did counting grains at different heights in a drop of water weigh a molecule?
    Perrin, 1908: grains settle into a tiny atmosphere; its height gives kT, and Avogadro's number.
  • How do lipid membranes assemble themselves? And what does heat, or acid, do to them?
    Coarse-grained lipids clustering into bilayer strips, from a random start.
  • Why does a helium balloon go flat faster than one filled with air?
    Effusion through tiny holes: leak rates in the ratio √(mass ratio), Graham's law.
  • How long would a smell take to cross a room on its own?
    Diffusion spreads as √t, a draught carries as t: two weeks against a minute.
  • How do computers predict which drug candidate binds best?
    Alchemical free-energy calculation, checked against counting binding events.
  • How do kidneys make urine four times saltier than blood?
    The countercurrent multiplier: small pumped steps, multiplied by a hairpin; ADH, alcohol, and the kangaroo rat.
  • Why are IV drips saline? What if they were pure water, or much saltier?
    A model cell swelling in water and shrinking in brine.
  • Why do reactions roughly double in speed every 10 °C?
    Escapes over an energy barrier counted at four temperatures: Arrhenius's law, cooking and insulin.
  • How does a buffer hold blood's pH steady when acid is added?
    Protons binding to buffer anions in a titration across 108 boxes; the law of mass action.
  • How can a mask stop particles much smaller than its holes?
    Aerosols carried through a fibre filter by a fan: diffusion, inertia and interception.
  • How does a PCR test copy DNA? Why do its strands come apart when heated?
    Bead strands pairing and melting: one bond is weak, many together hold to a length-dependent melting point.
