Molecular dynamics in your browser, on the Brownie engine

Micrographia

Questions about heat, entropy and everyday physics, each answered with a live simulation of the molecules involved, running on your GPU.

  • Why can't you un-stir cream from coffee, when every collision can run backwards?
    Exact time reversal, and what one flipped bit does to it.
  • Could all the air in your room rush into one corner? How long would you wait?
    768 boxes of gas counting how often every molecule is on one side.
  • Why is one molecule's energy distributed exponentially when the whole gas has a fixed total?
    The Boltzmann distribution emerging from an isolated gas, and Maxwell's speeds.
  • At the same temperature, does a heavy molecule have less energy than a light one?
    Equipartition, from light molecules to bowling balls, and Brownian motion.
  • Can heat ever flow from cold to hot?
    128 tiny systems between two heat baths, and the fluctuation theorem.
  • Why don't oil and water mix?
    Attraction, entropy and temperature in a separating mixture.
  • Why is osmosis an entropic force? Where does the push actually come from?
    A semi-permeable membrane, solute molecules bouncing off it, and van 't Hoff's law.
  • Why does a bike pump get hot, and a spray can cold?
    A piston doing work on a gas: the adiabat, fast compression, and heat-conducting walls.
  • Why does a stretched rubber band pull back, and why does it get warm?
    Polymer chains as entropic springs: a pull proportional to temperature.
  • Why does air pressure fall with height? And is it really colder up high?
    A gas column under gravity: exponential profiles per species, and a uniform temperature.
  • Why is the Carnot engine the most efficient possible? And how can a heat pump beat 100%?
    A gas engine run cycle by cycle, with every joule of heat and work booked.
  • Why does the boiling point rise with pressure? (And why does a pressure cooker cook faster?)
    Evaporation as escape over an energy barrier, and the Clausius–Clapeyron line.
  • How do lipid membranes assemble themselves? And what does heat, or acid, do to them?
    Coarse-grained lipids clustering into bilayer strips, from a random start.
  • Why does a helium balloon go flat faster than one filled with air?
    Effusion through tiny holes: leak rates in the ratio √(mass ratio), Graham's law.
  • How long would a smell take to cross a room on its own?
    Diffusion spreads as √t, a draught carries as t: two weeks against a minute.
  • How do computers predict which drug candidate binds best?
    Alchemical free-energy calculation, checked against counting binding events.
  • Why are IV drips saline? What if they were pure water, or much saltier?
    A model cell swelling in water and shrinking in brine.
  • Why do reactions roughly double in speed every 10 °C?
    Escapes over an energy barrier counted at four temperatures: Arrhenius's law, cooking and insulin.
  • How does a buffer hold blood's pH steady when acid is added?
    Protons binding to buffer anions in a titration across 108 boxes; the law of mass action.
  • How can a mask stop particles much smaller than its holes?
    Aerosols carried through a fibre filter by a fan: diffusion, inertia and interception.
  • How does a PCR test copy DNA? Why do its strands come apart when heated?
    Bead strands pairing and melting: one bond is weak, many together hold to a length-dependent melting point.
